This utility library turns Google Scholar into a structured, queryable citation graph rather than a temperamental web page. It retrieves publications, traces who cites whom across multiple generations, and persists the results in a carefully layered cache—so large-scale citation analysis becomes reproducible, inspectable, and fast instead of brittle and repetitive.

At its core, the library treats academic literature as a graph: a paper is a node, citations are edges, and each traversal step can be constrained, cached, validated, and resumed. It supports both “needle” queries (precise single-paper lookups) and “nets” (iterative searches filtered by author, year, or venue), automatically filling in missing metadata when higher-fidelity records become available.

A major design goal is resilience. Google Scholar is rate-limited, proxy-hostile, and occasionally fickle. This library compensates with automatic proxy rotation, retry logic, adaptive timeouts, and graceful recovery from hard failures—while keeping a persistent on-disk cache to minimize redundant requests.

The result is a tool aimed at researchers, bibliometric analysts, and technically inclined scholars who want citation networks at scale without turning every experiment into a multi-day scrape.

### Highlights

**Multi-level citation graph traversal**
Builds citation networks to arbitrary depth (papers → citing papers → citing those papers), tracking per-level and cumulative citation counts (internally modeled as structured TypedDict records).

**Flexible publication querying**
Supports exact-title lookups, expression-based title searches, and iterator-driven result streams (wrapping scholarly.search_pubs and search_single_pub with retry-aware call guards).

**Smart, layered caching system**
Query results, filled publication metadata, per-paper “cited by” lists, and full citation graphs are cached independently using deterministic, URL-safe filenames (base64/base85 encoding + metadata hashing). Cache behavior is controlled via a bitmask flag system rather than ad hoc booleans.

**Robust deduplication & validation**
Publications are matched and merged using multiple identifiers (bib_id, cites_id, author_pub_id, URLs), with conservative overwrite rules to prevent higher-quality data from being replaced by partial results.

**Failure-tolerant scraping**
Automatically rotates free proxies, retries failed calls up to configurable limits, and recovers transparently from MaxTriesExceededException without corrupting cache state.

**Traceable execution**
Dual logging to console and file provides detailed insight into query paths, cache hits, and error recovery—useful both for debugging and for auditability of results.
